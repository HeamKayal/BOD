Undiluted Sample Calculation

Use this when BOD is expected to be less than 5 mg/l (no dilution needed)

Calculation: BOD (mg/l) = Initial DO - Final DO

Diluted Sample Calculation

Use this when sample is diluted but not seeded

Calculation: BOD (mg/l) = (D₁ - D₂) × (1000 / P)
Where:
D₁ = Initial DO of sample
D₂ = Final DO of sample
P = Volume of sample (in ml, diluted to 1 litre)
1000 / P = Dilution factor (since total volume is 1 litre)

Seeded Sample Calculation

Use this when dilution water is seeded

Calculation: BOD (mg/l) = [D₁ - D₂ - (B₁ - B₂)f] × (1000 / P)
Where:
D₁ = Initial DO of sample
D₂ = Final DO of sample
B₁ = Initial DO of seed control
B₂ = Final DO of seed control
f = Ratio of seed in diluted sample to seed in control
P = Volume of sample (in ml, diluted to 1 litre)
1000 / P = Dilution factor (since total volume is 1 litre)

Glucose-Glutamic Acid Check

Standard check solution (150 mg glucose and 150 mg glutamic acid per litre, typically 20 ml diluted to 1 litre)

Calculation: BOD (mg/l) = [D₁ - D₂ - (B₁ - B₂)f] × (1000 / P)
Where:
D₁ = Initial DO of check solution
D₂ = Final DO of check solution
B₁ = Initial DO of seed control
B₂ = Final DO of seed control
f = Ratio of seed in check solution to seed in control
P = Volume of sample (in ml, diluted to 1 litre)
1000 / P = Dilution factor (since total volume is 1 litre)
Expected range: 200 ± 37 mg/l (157–237 mg/l)
If the BOD value is outside this range, reject any BOD determinations made with the seed and dilution water and investigate the problem.

Knowledge Information: BOD Analysis

Topic Details
Interferences in BOD Analysis BOD analysis measures the oxygen consumed by microorganisms while decomposing organic matter in water. Several factors can interfere with accurate results:

- Toxic Substances: The presence of toxic substances such as heavy metals (e.g., lead, mercury), pesticides, or industrial chemicals (e.g., sanitizers, antibiotics) can suppress microbial activity, leading to lower BOD values. For example, chlorination disinfection in sewage treatment or odor-control chemicals in sanitary waste can inhibit bacteria.

- Nitrification: The oxidation of ammonia to nitrate by nitrifying bacteria consumes oxygen, which can inflate BOD values if not controlled. To avoid this, nitrification inhibitors like allylthiourea (ATU) are often added during testing.

- Mineral Oxidation: Oxidation of inorganic materials like sulfides or ferrous iron can consume dissolved oxygen, interfering with the measurement of oxygen used for organic decomposition.

- Oxygenation: Exposure to atmospheric oxygen during sample handling or testing can artificially increase dissolved oxygen levels, skewing results. BOD bottles must be airtight to prevent this.

- Incomplete Metabolism: Complex organic compounds may not fully degrade within the standard 5-day incubation period, leading to an underestimation of BOD.

- Contamination: External organic matter or microorganisms introduced during sample collection or handling can elevate BOD values.

- Microbial Variability: The metabolic activity of microorganisms varies depending on their health, population, and adaptation to the sample. High microbial activity can overestimate BOD, while low activity can underestimate it.

- pH and Temperature: Extreme pH or deviations from the standard 20°C incubation temperature can affect microbial activity, altering BOD results. The BOD Calculator adjusts pH to 7.0 ± 0.2 to mitigate this.
Uses of BOD Analysis BOD analysis is a critical tool in environmental science and wastewater management, with the following applications:

- Water Quality Assessment: BOD indicates the level of organic pollution in water bodies. High BOD values suggest significant biodegradable organic matter, which can deplete oxygen and harm aquatic life.

- Wastewater Treatment Monitoring: BOD is used to evaluate the efficiency of wastewater treatment plants. It measures waste loads, the effectiveness of BOD removal, and the quality of effluent discharged into water bodies. The BOD Calculator supports this by providing calculations for undiluted, diluted, and seeded samples.

- Designing Treatment Systems: BOD data helps design wastewater treatment plants by estimating the organic load and required treatment capacity. The ratio of BOD to COD (Chemical Oxygen Demand) indicates the biodegradable fraction of effluent, aiding in plant sizing.

- Regulatory Compliance: BOD is used to ensure compliance with environmental regulations by monitoring effluent discharge. It helps assess the short-term impact of wastewater on receiving waters.

- Self-Purification Studies: BOD measures the self-purification capacity of streams, determining how much dilution water is needed to safely dispose of sewage.

- Glucose-Glutamic Acid Check: As implemented in the BOD Calculator, this check validates the reliability of BOD tests by ensuring results fall within the expected range (157–237 mg/L for a standard solution), confirming the quality of seed and dilution water.

- Industrial Applications: BOD is used in industries like pharmaceuticals and medical research to measure oxygen consumption by cell cultures, and in food processing to assess waste loads.

Standard Limits for BOD

Sample Name BOD Limit (mg/L)
Drinking Water <1 mg/L
Treated Sewage (Direct Discharge to Environment, e.g., U.S. NPDES, India CPCB) 30 mg/L
Untreated or Partially Treated Sewage (Discharge to Municipal Sewer) 250–350 mg/L
Receiving Waters (e.g., Rivers) <5 mg/L (CPCB: <3 mg/L for drinking after treatment, <6 mg/L for bathing)
Industrial Wastewater (e.g., Dairy) <8 mg/L (post-treatment)
Note: Limits vary by country, region, and purpose. They may be adjusted based on the COD/BOD ratio (indicating biodegradability) and local environmental conditions. Always refer to local regulations for precise standards.

Expression of Results

BOD is expressed as mg/l, 3 days at 27°C and rounded off as follows:

  • 0 to 10 mg/l: round to first decimal place
  • Above 10 mg/l: round to whole number
